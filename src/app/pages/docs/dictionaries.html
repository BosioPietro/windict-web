Dictionaries and network use

Where definitions and translations come from, and what WinDict sends over the network.

The dictionaries

  • English comes from Open English WordNet, over 150,000 words and phrases, which is built into the executable. Lookups in it don't use the network and take under a millisecond.
  • Italian comes from Wikizionario, about 67,000 words. It's downloaded once, about 8 MB, when it's chosen under Languages in the settings, and kept in %LOCALAPPDATA%\WinDict\dicts.
  • The online dictionary is used for pronunciations and for words the built-in ones don't have: Wiktionary, through dictionaryapi.dev, and Wikizionario for Italian.

With both languages chosen, a word is looked up in them in the order they're listed, English first.

What goes over the network

  • A word you look up, to the online dictionary, only when the built-in one doesn't have it or for its pronunciation. Turn off Online dictionary in the settings to never send one.
  • Text you translate, to Google Translate or MyMemory, only when you ask for a translation.
  • Once a day, a request to GitHub for the latest version. Turn off Check for updates to stop it.

Answers are kept for a while, so asking again doesn't go over the network a second time.

Memory

WinDict uses about 4 MB of memory while idle. The dictionaries are read in place: Windows only loads the few parts a lookup touches.

Credits

  • English definitions: Open English WordNet (CC BY 4.0, derived from Princeton WordNet).
  • Online English lookups: Wiktionary, through dictionaryapi.dev, falling back to Wiktionary directly.
  • Italian dictionary and online lookups: Wikizionario, through wiktextract, falling back to Wikizionario's own API (CC BY-SA 4.0).
  • Translations: Google Translate's public web endpoint, which is unofficial and may change, falling back to MyMemory.
