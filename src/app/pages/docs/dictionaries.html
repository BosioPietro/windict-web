Dictionaries and network use

Where definitions and translations come from, and what WinDict sends over the network.

The dictionaries

WinDict looks words up in as many languages as you like, offline. English is built in; every other language is a dictionary of its own, downloaded once when you choose it, in the introduction or under Languages in the settings, and kept in %LOCALAPPDATA%\WinDict\dicts.

Language From Words Download
English Open English WordNet Over 150,000 Built in
Italiano Wikizionario About 75,000 About 8 MB
Español Wikcionario About 135,000 About 17 MB
Français Wiktionnaire About 490,000 About 89 MB

More languages are coming: each new dictionary arrives with an update, ready to be picked in the settings.

With several languages chosen, a word is looked up in each in the order they're listed, English first. Lookups in a dictionary on your PC don't use the network and take under a millisecond.

The online dictionary is used for pronunciations and for words no dictionary on your PC has: each language's own Wiktionary, and dictionaryapi.dev for English.

What goes over the network

  • A word you look up, to the online dictionary, only when no dictionary on your PC has it, or for its pronunciation. Turn off Online dictionary in the settings to never send one.
  • Text you translate, to Google Translate or MyMemory, only when you ask for a translation.
  • Once a day, a request to GitHub for the latest version. Turn off Check for updates to stop it.

Answers are kept for a while, so asking again doesn't go over the network a second time.

Memory

WinDict uses about 4 MB of memory while idle. The dictionaries are read in place: Windows only loads the few parts a lookup touches.
