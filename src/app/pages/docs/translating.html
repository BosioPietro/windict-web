Translating

Translate a word, a phrase or a whole passage without leaving what you're reading.

Translating the selection

Select a word, a phrase or a whole passage, of up to 500 characters, and press : the card opens on the translation. For a single word the dictionary has, Back shows its definition.

On a definition, the translate button at the top right of the card, or , shows a translation of the word instead. Press it again to go back to the definition.

The languages are at the top of the card. Click one to pick another, typing to filter the list, or the arrows between them to swap them.

Typing the text to translate

With nothing selected, opens a box to type the text into, with the languages under it, as on the translation card. As you type, WinDict tells the language from its words, using the dictionaries you've chosen, and picks the languages:

  • with two dictionary languages, from the one you're typing in into the other;
  • otherwise, between the default languages in the settings, into the one you're not typing in;
  • when the language can't be told, the default way.

Click a language to pick another one, or the arrows to swap them; from then on they stay as you set them. Press to translate; Back on the translation brings the box back as you left it.

Where translations come from

Translations come from Google Translate's public web endpoint, falling back to MyMemory when it can't answer. They need the online dictionary to be on, in the settings, and are fetched only when you ask for one.

MyMemory has a daily limit on free translations. An e-mail address under Translation → MyMemory email in the settings raises it; it's sent only to MyMemory, with the text translated.
