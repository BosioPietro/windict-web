Looking up words

Select a word in any app and press : its definition opens in a card next to the selection.

What the card shows

The card lists the word's senses under each part of speech it can be, each with an example sentence where the dictionary has one. You can select a word or a short phrase, of up to four words, or a whole passage to gloss.

  • Inflected forms are looked up by their base word, so selecting “ran” shows “run”, and the card says what it's the form of.
  • Similar words and, where there are any, opposite ones (“good”: “bad”) are listed under each part of speech. Clicking one opens its definition in the same card.
  • To go back, use the arrow next to the word, , or the mouse's back button.
  • When a word isn't in the dictionary, the card suggests the closest one it has: “Did you mean…?”

People, places and brands

Names the dictionaries skip, like “Ada Lovelace”, “Kyoto” or “Spotify”, are answered from Wikipedia: the card shows what the article is about, its first few sentences and its picture, with a link to read the rest. When a name could be several things, the card says Wikipedia has several articles by it.

When a word you look up has an article of its own, a W button fades in between the translate and pin buttons, and opens it in your browser. Wikipedia is asked in the languages you look words up in, and only while the online dictionary is on; turn off Wikipedia in the settings to leave it out.

Glossing a passage

Select a whole passage instead, of up to 500 characters, and glosses it: the card shows the passage with its uncommon words underlined. Click one, or move to it with and press , to open its definition in the same card; Back comes back to the passage.

  • WinDict tells the passage's language from its words, in any of the 22 it translates, with a list of each one's common words built in. Names, numbers and words in capitals are left alone.
  • The translate button translates the whole passage, and puts the translation in place of the selection.

Hearing a word

The speaker button after the word says it out loud, in the word's own language, with a voice Windows has for it. It shows only when there's one: voices for more languages can be added in Windows Settings, under Time & language → Speech.

Typing a word

With nothing selected, opens a box to type a word into instead, for text that can't be selected, such as in an image, a protected PDF or a game.

  • As you type, up to five suggestions from the offline dictionary appear under the box. Pick one with or , or click it.
  • looks up what's in the box. When the offline dictionary has nothing like it, Enter searches online.
  • closes the box.

Reading text off the screen

For text that can't be selected or is too long to type, the scan button by the box's heading reads it off the screen. The card steps aside: drag over the text, and the box comes back beside it with the text typed in. cancels.

WinDict reads with the text recognition Windows installs with each language, and in the box to translate, it picks the language it found the text in. The button shows only when Windows has text recognition for at least one language.

Pinning a card

The pin button at the top right of the card keeps it open. A pinned card stays above every other window until you close it, and you can drag it wherever you like. New lookups open cards of their own, so you can keep several pinned side by side.

Once you've clicked a pinned card, it takes the keyboard like any window: or closes it, and the card's other keys work as usual. The pin button unpins it again.

Selecting and copying

The card's text can be selected like a page's: drag over it, or double-click a word. copies what's selected.

With nothing selected, copies the word, or the translation when the card shows one. With a similar word picked with , it copies that word instead.

Text size

To make the card's text larger or smaller, press or , or hold and turn the mouse wheel over the card. goes back to the usual size. The size goes from 80% to 200%, is kept, and is also under Text size in the settings. The settings window follows the same size, and the same keys work there.

How WinDict reads the selection

WinDict reads the selection through UI Automation, the way screen readers do, so nothing is copied and the clipboard isn't touched. Some apps don't share their text that way: in those, WinDict copies the selection instead, then puts back what was on the clipboard. How it reads is under Read selections in the settings.
